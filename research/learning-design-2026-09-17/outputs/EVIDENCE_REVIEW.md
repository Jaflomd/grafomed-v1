# Targeted evidence review

Date: 2026-09-17. Purpose: improve product design, not establish Grafo Med efficacy. Pillars: amauta-education (primary), research, ai-enhancement.

## Method and limits

Targeted searches covered adaptive versus fixed medical education, spaced education, explicit instruction, worked examples and fading, feedback, clinical reasoning transfer, and ECG interleaving. Accessible original-study records were preferred. Ten primary educational studies are retained below, including nonmedical mechanism studies and null or limiting results. This is neither exhaustive nor a systematic review; no formal risk-of-bias appraisal, meta-analysis or certainty rating was performed.

Consensus searches failed because the account's monthly quota was exhausted; no additional usage was authorized or enabled. Retrieval continued through PubMed, publisher/author institutional records and web search. Some direct PubMed pages returned empty extraction and a PMC page presented a browser check. The evidence below is predominantly abstract-level, not represented as full-text appraisal. Repeated AI interpretations are not independent evidence. Engineering and education reviewers supplied design critiques, not learner outcomes.

## Retained studies

### E1. Renkl et al., 2002 — fading worked examples

*From example study to problem solving: Smooth transitions help learning.* [Author institutional record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/from-example-study-to-problem-solving-smooth-transitions-help-lea/).

One field and two laboratory experiments supported gradual withdrawal of worked steps, at least for near transfer. Access: institutional publication record/abstract reviewed by educational reviewer. These are structured learning tasks, not a validation of medical clinical transfer. Design inference: offer completion problems as a bridge; do not require an identical sequence for all learners.

### E2. Kalyuga et al., 2001 — expertise can reverse the example advantage

*When Problem Solving Is Superior to Studying Worked Examples.* DOI: [10.1037/0022-0663.93.3.579](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.93.3.579). [Author-deposited original record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/49249052_When_Problem_Solving_Is_Superior_to_Studying_Worked_Examples).

Experiments with mechanical apprentices found an initial worked-example advantage that changed as expertise increased. Access: original abstract and institutional metadata verified by educational reviewer; publisher full text not accessed. Population is nonmedical. Design inference: allow relevant demonstrated prior knowledge to shorten scaffolding; one correct response does not establish expertise.

### E3. Heitzmann et al., 2015 — adaptable feedback, not mandatory prompts

*Enhancing diagnostic competence with self-explanation prompts and adaptable feedback.* Medical Education. DOI: [10.1111/medu.12778](https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.12778). [PubMed PMID 26383071](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26383071/).

Randomized factorial study, 103 medical students: learner-selectable feedback depth improved strategic and decision-oriented practical knowledge; additional self-explanation prompts did not add benefit. Access: PubMed abstract/metadata. Adaptable here means learner control, not an algorithm diagnosing cognitive deficits. Design inference: optional feedback depth is plausible; prompting after every action is not warranted.

### E4. Chamberland et al., 2019 — feedback gains do not establish far transfer

*Does providing the correct diagnosis as feedback after self-explanation improve medical students diagnostic performance?* BMC Medical Education 19:194. DOI: [10.1186/s12909-019-1638-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-019-1638-3). [PubMed PMID 31185971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31185971/).

94 third-year students, three randomized feedback conditions, one-week test: correct-diagnosis feedback improved near-transfer performance relative to no feedback; no significant immediate-versus-delayed or far-transfer differences. Access: PubMed abstract/metadata. Nonsignificance does not demonstrate equivalence. Design inference: track near/far context performance separately; feedback timing depends on assessment purpose, not a universal learning advantage.

### E5. Kerfoot, 2010 — adaptive spacing may improve efficiency

*Adaptive spaced education improves learning efficiency: a randomized controlled trial.* Journal of Urology 183:678–681. DOI: [10.1016/j.juro.2009.10.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2009.10.005). [PubMed PMID 20022032](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20022032/).

62 third-year medical students; adaptive versus nonadaptive delivery of 40 urology items. Adaptive learners received fewer questions; test-score differences were not significant. Access: PubMed abstract/metadata. This does not establish score equivalence or a transferable numerical efficiency gain for Grafo Med. Design inference: compare learning and time/burden separately, not just total correct responses.

### E6. Faber et al., 2024 — adaptive scaffolding can show no advantage

*Effects of adaptive scaffolding on performance, cognitive load and engagement in game-based learning: a randomized controlled trial.* BMC Medical Education 24:943. [PubMed PMID 39210381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39210381/).

62 students from three Dutch universities, matched-pair randomized adaptive versus yoked nonadaptive support in emergency game scenarios. The abstract reports no discernible group differences in the measured outcomes. Access: primary PubMed search-extracted abstract; direct extraction/full-text access unsuccessful. Design inference: adaptation needs comparison with a good nonadaptive route; do not buy complexity on the strength of the label alone. A null result in this design does not establish that all personalization is ineffective.

### E7. Grad et al., 2021 — reminders are not sufficient

*Does spaced education improve clinical knowledge among Family Medicine residents? A cluster randomized controlled trial.* Advances in Health Sciences Education 26:771–783. DOI: [10.1007/s10459-020-10020-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-020-10020-z). [PubMed PMID 33389233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33389233/).

12 Canadian programs; 335 intervention and 299 control residents consented. The app-alert intervention did not significantly improve completion; certification-score confidence intervals did not exceed the prespecified four-point benefit threshold. Access: PubMed abstract/metadata. The intervention encouraged spacing through alerts; it is not a clean test disproving retrieval practice. Design inference: notifications and streaks are not substitutes for completed, useful learning.

### E8. Monteiro et al., 2017 — interleaving is not universally superior

*Evaluating the effect of instruction and practice schedule on the acquisition of ECG interpretation skills.* Perspectives on Medical Education 6:237–245. DOI: [10.1007/s40037-017-0365-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40037-017-0365-x). [PubMed PMID 28744821](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28744821/).

80 first-year medical students, 2×2 instruction/practice schedule study. Distributed instruction outperformed massed instruction; blocked practice exceeded mixed practice on the delayed final test. Access: PubMed search-extracted abstract/metadata. Task-specific result, not a general prohibition on interleaving. Design inference: test selective contrast after foundations rather than mix every topic from the start.

### E9. Brush et al., 2019 — explicit concepts can matter

*Effect of Teaching Bayesian Methods Using Learning by Concept vs Learning by Example on Medical Students' Ability to Estimate Probability of a Diagnosis: A Randomized Clinical Trial.* JAMA Network Open 2:e1918023. DOI: [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.18023](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2019.18023). [PubMed PMID 31860107](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31860107/).

61 medical students; explicit conceptual instruction, example experience with feedback, or control. Concept instruction produced closer probability estimates in the tested task. Access: PubMed abstract/metadata. Narrow Bayesian estimation task; it does not prove that any short video improves all clinical reasoning. Design inference: protect explicit instruction rather than assume assessment plus feedback can replace it.

### E10. Raupach et al., 2016 — practice beyond rereading

*Test-enhanced learning of clinical reasoning: a crossover randomised trial.* Medical Education 50:711–720. DOI: [10.1111/medu.13069](https://doi.org/10.1111/medu.13069). [PubMed PMID 27295475](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27295475/).

87 of 124 eligible fourth-year students contributed complete longitudinal data. Key-feature case testing exceeded reading the same case material on a nine-month written assessment. Access: primary PubMed abstract/metadata via search extraction. Attrition and outcome-format similarity limit interpretation; no patient outcomes were established. Design inference: include active retrieval/decision practice after teaching, without claiming workplace competence from written performance.

## Synthesis boundary

These studies justify candidate mechanisms and useful cautions, not the combined product. The proposed graph, event model and version control are engineering choices; the precise lesson length, route thresholds, mastery criteria, review intervals and scenario counts remain unvalidated design parameters. No study here supports certifying independent clinical practice from app completion. Test the teaching loop first, then the incremental value of adaptation.
